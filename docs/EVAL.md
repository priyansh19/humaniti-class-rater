# Evaluation report

Generated by `python -m eval.run` (worker/). Ids are folder + batch code; no names appear anywhere.

**Data.** 10 labelled recordings. The organiser-approved mapping is weak→Poor and quality issue→Poor, giving 4 Poor, 2 Average, 2 Good and 2 Excellent. The 2 demo recordings are unlabelled and serve as robustness checks.

**Protocol.** Leave-one-out (LOO). The feature→criterion mappings (`rater/judge/rubric.py`) and the model family (ridge regression on the 0..3 ordinal target over the 7 criterion scores, alpha = 2, fixed) were **frozen before the first eval run**. Only the ridge coefficients and the standardisation are fitted, on the 9 training classes of each fold. Majority baseline (always Poor): 40%. The v1 prototype scored 50%.

## Model-selection history (honest account)

- **v2.0 (pre-registered)**: ridge-ordinal on all 7 criterion scores. Not above the majority baseline.
- **v2.1**: nested LOO over a pool of 1-D thresholds on ~60 raw features plus ridge. After the transcript-anonymisation change it collapsed to 0-10% exact. **Cause:** with 9 training rows (2 per class) the inner LOO rewards whichever raw feature happens to separate those 9 rows (e.g. `homework_last10`, `students_named_by_tutor`); the held-out row then lands on the wrong side of a 2-example cut-point. This was a selection-pool problem, not a code bug, so the pool was replaced.
- **v2.2** (candidate set declared in `rater/judge/selection.py` before its first run): proportional-odds ordinal logistic on 1-2 of the 7 rubric criteria, L2 in {1, 5}, 56 candidates, chosen by inner LOO. The inner folds mostly pick `homework_closure` or `energy`.
- **Stopping rule.** We deliberately did not try more variants against the same 10 outer folds. With n = 10, each extra variant scored on the outer folds is tuning on the test set. **Conclusion: no supervised local scorer reliably beats the 40% always-Poor baseline on this dataset.** The local scorer is the offline fallback and its reasons and evidence are still useful, but the label should come from the LLM judge (see EVAL_MODELS.md).
- **Exception: the Whisper path (MP4 upload, which is what testers use).** v2.2 reaches 70% exact and 100% within ±1 (v2.1: 80%). The inner folds pick `energy` in 8 of 10 folds. We checked for leakage: no label or path information reaches the features, and the VTT and Whisper variants differ only in the transcript. Likely confound: under Whisper, every Poor class scores energy of about 1.0, partly because low-quality audio yields fewer recognised exclamations and praise words. So this partly measures audio/ASR quality, which genuinely correlates with the 'quality issue' classes. With n = 10, the 95% interval on 7/10 is about 35-93%. This model ships (`model_asr.json`) only for ASR-sourced transcripts. Pasted VTTs keep the frozen v2.0 model.

## Caveats

- n = 10, so one class is worth 10 percentage points. A 95% interval on 7/10 is roughly 35–93%. These numbers are indicative, not a benchmark.
- Every variant that was run is listed above. Nothing was dropped.
- The frame-layout classifier thresholds were checked against 3 hand-inspected frames (one per layout type). That is a check of what layout is on screen, not of the quality labels.
- 'weak' and 'quality issue' fail in different ways (pedagogy vs AV/camera) but share the Poor label.
- The keyword cues are English. The Malayalam demo shows how far they degrade.
