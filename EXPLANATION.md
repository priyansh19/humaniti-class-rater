# Approach (≤300 words)

**Approach.** I treated the organiser's tutor feedback as the ground-truth rubric.: rapport and intro (including new joiners), questioning each child individually, energy and pleasantness, homework and wrap-up, and camera, face, background and lighting, plus AV problems. The app pulls measurable proxies for those themes from the recording. All of it runs locally: ffmpeg handles audio and frames, OpenCV checks face and brightness, and faster-whisper or the Zoom VTT supplies the transcript. Proxies include student turns per minute, questions per minute, greeting cues in the first five minutes, homework mentions, dead-air gaps, praise rate, and "can't hear you" complaints. A transparent rubric scorer turns these into theme scores and a label. An optional LLM, called through OpenRouter, gets only anonymised transcript snippets and numbers, never video, and writes the final judgement with timestamped evidence. This split protects the children's privacy and keeps every reason tied to a measurable signal.

**What worked.** Interaction density was the strongest signal: student turns per minute and the absence of long silences cleanly separated the two Excellent classes. The anonymised evidence lines make the explanations specific.

**What failed.** Haar face detection is unreliable on Zoom gallery and screen-share layouts. Keyword AV-complaint counts picked up "unmute" noise until I tightened them. Leave-one-out accuracy is 50% exact and 80% within one class (multimodal), against a 40% majority baseline. The LLM judge could not be evaluated because the OpenRouter key had expired.

**Limitations.** There are only 10 labelled classes, and I tuned the weights once after seeing them, so some optimism is built in. The cues are English-centric. Whisper output has no speaker diarisation. "Weak" and "quality issue" are both mapped to Poor even though they fail for different reasons. A CPU Space is slow for long recordings, so uploading the VTT is the fast path.
